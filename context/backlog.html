Is 11 m the right spacing between fixes?

fix-spacing  ·  measured

11 m is not a city-versus-trail number at all — it is a fraction of the bucket. The target exists so that no 15 m bucket goes unmeasured. Judged against that, it holds below about 6 m/s and fails above it, in both worlds equally.

And current trail riding is slower than street riding, so spacing there is already finer than the target. Fix frequency is almost certainly not what is limiting trail detail.

Two things fall out of this. First, the phone under-delivers against its own request. At 7 m/s the computed interval is about 1.57 s, which should give 11 m; the measured gap is 14.2 m, implying the operating system is handing over fixes roughly 2 s apart. The interval is a request, not a contract.

Second, there is a hard floor that no tuning can move. MIN_INTERVAL_MS is 1000 because GPS is a 1 Hz device. At 10 m/s the best achievable spacing is 10 m, and at 15 m/s it is 15 m — a whole bucket. Descents are the fastest part of any ride, so the fastest ground is always the worst sampled, and the interval knob cannot fix it.

Ideas, undecided

  • Accept that above ~8 m/s buckets get skipped, and instead interpolate between fixes when the gap exceeds one bucket. Cheap, but it invents data, and the whole model has so far refused to do that.
  • Use the barometer's higher sample rate to fill the gaps. Height is what buckets store; position between two fixes 20 m apart is well approximated by the segment centreline anyway, since we already project onto it.
  • Leave the target alone and simply record how many buckets were skipped per ride as a quality figure. Measuring the damage before treating it fits how everything else here was decided.

Why a real green section inside an orange climb never shows up

slope-resolution  ·  answered what to do is open

The bucket size is not the culprit; the slope window is. Colour is computed from a least-squares line through six buckets — a 75 m baseline. Any change in gradient shorter than roughly 54 m is averaged into its surroundings before a colour is ever chosen.

Three smoothers stack, in this order

  1. The moving average over heights — about 3 seconds
    EMA_ALPHA = 0.3. At trail speeds of 2–3 m/s that is 6–9 m of ground; on a fast street descent it is closer to 20 m.
  2. The bucket — 15 m
    Every fix within ±7.5 m of a mark is averaged into one height. This sets the finest detail that can be stored.
  3. The slope window — 75 m
    SLOPE_WINDOW = 2 fits a line through the bucket at each end of the gap plus the two beyond each of those — six buckets. This sets the finest detail that can be seen, and it is five times coarser than the bucket.

The arithmetic, on your example

Take a 5% climb with a 15 m stretch at −2% in the middle of it — a real dip you can feel in the pedals. The window centred on that dip sees 15 m of −2% and 60 m of +5%:

Length of the dipSlope the window reportsColour drawnColour deserved
15 m+3.60%orangegreen
22 m+2.95%yellowgreen
30 m+2.20%yellowgreen
54 m−0.04%greengreen

Why the window cannot simply be shrunk

It was widened for a reason. About 0.15 m of barometer noise across a 15 m bucket is roughly 1% of slope error from a plain two-point difference — about the width of a colour band. That is what made a flat street come out speckled with oranges and blues. Narrow the window today and the speckle comes back.

The leverage is noise, not the window. Oversampling the barometer — already the first item on the main plan, and close to power-neutral — cuts noise roughly fourfold. That is what would buy the room to drop SLOPE_WINDOW to 1, a 30 m baseline, and roughly double the resolution without the speckle returning.

Two things worth fixing regardless. A line needs six buckets before any gap on it can get a full window. Of the 341 lines currently rendered, 205 — 60% — have five or fewer, so on most of the map the fit is silently running on three or four points.

Worse, 23 lines have exactly one bucket. With a single bucket the code cannot compute a slope at all and falls back to zero, which maps to the green band. Those 23 lines are painted "gentle descent" on no evidence whatsoever.

Ideas, undecided

  • Cut the barometer noise first, then narrow the window. The ordering matters — doing it the other way round just reintroduces a bug that was already paid for.
  • Make the window adaptive: narrow where a bucket has several rides behind it, wide where it has one. sample_count is already stored per bucket, so the information is there.
  • Give trails a smaller BUCKET_SIZE_M. Tempting, but it does nothing on its own — the 75 m window would still span twice as many smaller buckets and land in the same place.
  • Render one-bucket and low-confidence lines as a neutral grey rather than green, so "we don't know" stops looking like "gentle descent".

How should segments work on trails, where there are no intersections?

trail-segmentation  ·  measured open

Trail segments today are arbitrary. On roads a segment is a real thing — a block between two junctions. On a trail there is nothing to cut at, so the pipeline falls back to chopping the way into equal pieces of at most 150 m. Those boundaries mean nothing on the ground.

What the network looks like

footway13,30534.2 m133.7 m859

The road median of 87.5 m is a city block, which is the number topology gives you for free. Cycleways run longer and hit the cap far more often in proportion — and one single OSM way in the database was split into 144 pieces, with several others between 30 and 63.

Why arbitrary boundaries actually hurt

  • The coverage test loses its meaning. The gate accepts a run covering 70% of "the block". On a trail the denominator is a number the splitter invented, so 70% of it is not a fact about anything.
  • The stored bearing becomes nonsense. Each segment carries one straight-line bearing. Chop a switchback into 150 m chunks and a chunk's net bearing can point somewhere the rider never faced — which is precisely the failure fragment stitching exists to paper over.
  • A 150 m chunk is about ten buckets, wide enough to contain a full climb, crest and descent, which the 75 m slope window then smears across the crest.

Ideas, undecided

  • Cut at direction changes rather than at even intervals. Walk the way and start a new segment wherever cumulative heading has turned past some threshold. This attacks the root cause behind stitching instead of compensating for it, and every segment would then have a bearing that means something.
  • Use a shorter cap for trails than for roads — more, smaller pieces makes each bearing more honest, at the cost of more segments and more gate decisions.
  • Do not cut long trails at all: treat a named trail as one segment with a very long ruler. Simplest conceptually, but it breaks the coverage test in the other direction and makes every query heavier.
  • Whatever is chosen, it changes segment ids and requires a re-import and a full model rebuild. This is the most expensive item here to change later, which argues for deciding it before recording many more trail rides.

Why is there nothing on Ute Valley Regional Trail?

ute-valley  ·  answered — no bug

The trail is in the database, correctly classified, and has never been ridden with recording on. All nine of its segments exist as cycleway, none were folded into a parent road, and zero GPS fixes have ever landed within 30 m of any of them. There is nothing to fix.

Ute Valley Regional Trail sits about 650 m northeast of the BeaUTEiful Loop, which is one of the best-covered pieces of trail in the whole model. So the area is mapped and the matcher works there — that particular path just has not been ridden.

One real exclusion worth auditing, though. highway=track is on the excluded list in split_ways.mjs, alongside driveways and parking aisles. Any local trail tagged track in OpenStreetMap is invisible to this app and always will be, silently.

Worth one query against the raw Overpass extract to count how many rideable-looking track ways exist locally before deciding whether that exclusion is still right for a mountain-biking use case.

Should mountain and city tracking be separated?

one-pipeline-or-two  ·  open — leaning no

Leaning strongly toward one pipeline, parameterised by segment kind. Not because the two are similar — they clearly are not — but because a fork would have to cut a single ride in half.

The argument against forking

  • One ride crosses both worlds. You ride streets to the trailhead and streets home. A forked pipeline would need to decide, mid-session, which code path each fix belongs to — and that decision is itself a map-matching problem, so nothing has been made simpler.
  • The knobs that differ are already isolated. Everything under discussion is a named constant in one of three files. Varying them by kind is a lookup, not a second pipeline.
  • Two paths drift. The rebuild script already exists specifically so that reprocessing old rides goes through exactly the same code as a live save. Forking would create the same class of divergence that guard was written to prevent.

What genuinely differs, and would need to vary by kind

KnobTodayWhy a trail might want something else
MAX_SEGMENT_M150No intersections to cut at, so the cap is doing all the work
BUCKET_SIZE_M15Terrain changes over shorter distances than a city block
MAX_BEARING_DELTA_DEG45Switchbacks routinely exceed it; stitching is the current workaround
MIN_COVERAGE0.7Measured against an arbitrary denominator on trails

The catch: any per-kind value has to be applied identically at rebuild time, and a bucket already written under one setting is not comparable to one written under another. Whatever is chosen, it lands with a full rebuild — the same discipline that already applies to every constant marked "rebuild".

What would a dev view cost?

dev-view  ·  scoped not built

Four separate features, listed cheapest first. Everything below reads data that already exists; none of it changes how a ride is processed.

A · Bucket counts and run counts on the segment

Smallest. The /segments route already loads every bucket row for the viewport in order to build the lines, then discards the counts. Attaching bucket count and maximum sample_count per segment is a field on the existing response with no extra query. The only real work is a zoom-gated label layer on the map.

B · Where the fixes actually landed

Small. Needs one new read-only route returning a session's raw samples — no such endpoint exists today. Payload is trivial: the longest recent ride is 862 fixes. On the map it is a point layer with a toggle. Most valuable while the ride is fresh, since it shows scatter and dropouts directly rather than through their effects.

C · Tap a segment to see its buckets

Medium. A per-segment detail route returning every bucket — distance, stored mean height, how many rides are behind it — plus the coverage row and the matched runs. All three tables exist and are indexed. The cost is mostly the panel UI, not the data.

D · Why is there no line here?

Medium, and the one worth arguing for. Every save already computes a full diagnostic for each rejected run — span, coverage fraction, sample count, how many runs landed on the same segment, what the span would have been if stitched, and the gap in seconds to the previous run. Then it is deliberately thrown away: the field is marked diagnostics-only and stripped from the API response.

Persisting that to a table would turn the hardest question in this whole system — why is this street blank? — into something tappable. The computation is already written and already runs on every ride; only the table and the insert are missing.

Keep it off the hot path. Whatever gets built, it should be separate routes behind a flag and gated on zoom, so the normal viewport request keeps exactly the cost it has now. The one exception is A, which rides along on a query that already runs.

If only three things happen: decide trail segmentation before recording many more trail rides, because it is the only item here that gets more expensive with every ride. Persist the discard diagnostics, because the work is already done and thrown away. Cut barometer noise, because it is the one change that unlocks finer slope resolution rather than trading it against speckle.
